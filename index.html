How scoring works

Delivery comes from the audio: pace (words per minute), pauses of 0.3 s or more, vocal variety (how widely your pitch moves, in semitones), volume control, and filler words per minute.

Language comes from the transcript: average sentence length and hedges such as "I think" or "sort of".

Content is scored by Claude against a five-part rubric: structure, clarity of the main message, evidence and reasoning, fit with the audience, and the opening and close.

Each measure is scored 0–100 against the target range for the audience you choose. The overall score weights the three groups by what that audience cares about.

The ranges follow common speech-coaching guidance (for example, about 130–160 words per minute for a presentation). To compare against a specific talk you admire, score it here and save it as your reference talk.

Automatic transcripts often drop "um" and "uh", so filler counts may run low.

Your recording stays in your browser. When you ask for content feedback, only the transcript and the delivery numbers go to Claude, on your own Claude account. Scores are a rehearsal aid, not a verdict on you as a speaker.
